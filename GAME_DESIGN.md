# Game Design Document

*First-person open-world survival — working title.*

## Project Vision

Create a first-person open-world survival game where the player's camping van
or expedition truck is the only true safe place in the world.

The vehicle is not simply transportation. It is the player's home, shelter,
workshop, storage, kitchen, bedroom, and lifeline.

The emotional experience revolves around one simple rule:

> **Inside the vehicle, the player feels completely safe.**
> **Outside the vehicle, the player never feels safe.**

Every game mechanic should reinforce this contrast.

## Core Gameplay Loop

1. Drive through dangerous landscapes.
2. Spot a location worth exploring.
3. Decide whether the reward is worth the risk.
4. Park the vehicle.
5. Prepare equipment.
6. Open the door — the moment the player steps outside, tension immediately begins.
7. Search quickly for supplies.
8. Listen for distant sounds. Watch for movement.
9. Collect only what is necessary.
10. Return to the vehicle before danger overwhelms the player.
11. Close the door. Instantly feel relief.
12. Repair equipment. Cook food. Upgrade the vehicle. Sleep.
13. Plan the next destination.
14. Repeat.

The player should always think twice before leaving the vehicle.

## World

The game takes place years after civilization collapsed. Nature has reclaimed
civilization. The world should feel beautiful but constantly threatening, and
every location contains useful resources and hidden danger.

Location types:

- Large forests
- Fog-covered highways
- Abandoned cities
- Small villages
- Gas stations
- Military checkpoints
- Campgrounds
- Underground bunkers
- Factories
- Power stations
- Remote cabins
- Mountains
- Snow regions
- Swamps

## Safe Zone: The Vehicle

The camping vehicle is the player's only guaranteed safe zone. Once the player
enters the vehicle and closes the doors, enemies cannot attack.

Inside the vehicle the player can:

- Sleep and save progress
- Cook meals and purify water
- Repair weapons and equipment
- Craft supplies
- Organize inventory
- Charge batteries
- Upgrade vehicle systems
- Listen to the weather
- Monitor nearby threats through cameras or windows
- Read maps and plan routes
- Decorate and personalize the interior

Over time the vehicle should genuinely feel like home.

## Outside World

The outside world should constantly create anxiety.

Possible dangers include:

- Zombies
- Mutated creatures
- Wolves, bears, and wild dogs
- Bandits and hostile survivors
- Environmental hazards
- Radiation zones
- Poisonous plants
- Collapsed structures

Enemy behavior varies:

- Some enemies patrol.
- Some hunt using sound; some hunt using sight.
- Some only appear during specific weather.
- Some become extremely aggressive at night.

The player should never know exactly what is waiting outside.

## Day and Night Cycle

**Daytime** is the safest time to explore:

- Visibility is good.
- Animals are easier to avoid.
- Resources are easier to locate.
- Most enemies are slower and less aggressive.

**Night** completely changes the experience:

- Darkness limits visibility.
- Stronger creatures emerge and zombie activity increases.
- Predators become more aggressive.
- Unusual sounds become common.
- Weather becomes harder to predict.
- Travel becomes significantly more dangerous.

Players should naturally prefer traveling during daylight and only venture out
at night when absolutely necessary.

## Dynamic Weather

Weather should directly affect gameplay — never cosmetic, always affecting
survival:

- **Heavy rain** reduces visibility.
- **Fog** hides enemies until they are close.
- **Thunder** masks player movement but also hides approaching threats.
- **Snow** slows movement.
- **Heat** causes dehydration.
- **Wind** changes the direction of sound.
- **Storms** can damage roads and block paths.

## Survival Systems

The player manages:

- Fuel
- Food and water
- Medicine
- Vehicle condition
- Battery power
- Engine temperature
- Tire wear
- Tools and repair parts
- Weapons and ammunition

Running out of fuel far from safety should be one of the most stressful
moments in the game.

## Exploration

The player leaves the vehicle only because survival demands it. Reasons
include:

- Finding food
- Collecting fuel
- Searching abandoned buildings
- Recovering medicine
- Finding replacement vehicle parts
- Collecting rare crafting materials
- Finding maps
- Discovering hidden bunkers
- Helping survivors
- Recovering valuable technology

Every expedition should feel meaningful.

## Vehicle Progression

The vehicle evolves throughout the game. Possible upgrades include:

**Drivetrain and power**

- Engine upgrades
- Larger fuel tanks
- Solar panels
- Battery systems
- Emergency generator

**Protection**

- Armor plating
- Reinforced windows
- Stronger doors
- Security cameras
- Automatic lighting

**Living systems**

- Water filtration
- Medical station
- Kitchen
- Heating and air conditioning

**Utility**

- Workbench
- Weapon storage
- Roof rack and extra storage
- Drone deployment system
- Weather scanner
- Navigation computer
- Communication radio

The player should become emotionally attached to the vehicle because it
represents survival.

## Dynamic World

The world should continuously evolve:

- Roads become blocked; bridges collapse.
- Animals migrate.
- Zombie hordes move.
- Bandits establish camps.
- Weather changes travel routes.
- Supplies slowly disappear.
- Random emergency events occur.

Every journey should feel different.

## Audio

Audio is critical. The player should constantly hear:

- Wind, rain, and branches moving
- Footsteps and animals
- Distant screams and metal noises
- Vehicle engine sounds and door locks

Inside the vehicle the audio should become calmer and comforting. Outside, the
audio should constantly create uncertainty.

## Visual Direction

Highly realistic graphics:

- Dense forests, thick fog, heavy rain, snowstorms
- Golden sunsets and pitch-black nights
- Warm interior lighting and a detailed vehicle interior
- Realistic reflections and natural lighting
- Photorealistic environments

Every location should encourage exploration while also making the player
nervous.

## Emotional Goal

The player should develop a real emotional attachment to the vehicle. It is
not simply a truck — it is the player's home. The player should experience
relief every time they return and close the door.

The game succeeds if players hesitate before opening that door, because they
know that once they step outside, survival is no longer guaranteed.
